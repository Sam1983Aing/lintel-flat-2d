# Living room, three looks, blind review (14 Oct 2026)

Outside review, judged only from the 21 pictures in `2026-10-14-living-looks-blind/` (A, B, C, seven views each). I did not open any other project file and do not know which version is which.

| Version | Materials | Light | Premium feel | Overall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 4 | 4.5 | 4 | **4** |
| B | 6 | 5.5 | 5.5 | **6** |
| C | 5.5 | 4.5 | 5 | **5** |

Anchors used: 3 obvious CG, 5 decent real-time render, 7 convincing at a glance, 8 reads as a photographed room in most places, 10 a photograph.

**Pick for a client:** B, because it is the only one where the oak and the board-marked concrete read as the real thing and the warm light makes the room feel lived in rather than lit.

---

## What the pictures show about the three

Read straight off the images (no other files):

- **A and C share the same light and colour balance.** Mean sRGB values from the same crops: ceiling A 170/158/156, C 168/156/155. Right-hand plaster A 146/143/151, C 144/141/150. Both lean lilac (blue channel above green on the walls).
- **B and C share the same textures.** Same board-marked concrete (identical tie marks and chips in `fire.jpg`), same oak figure, same bark, same logs, same fine-weave upholstery. B is warm (ceiling 188/164/138, floor under the chairs 116/90/69), C is cool (floor under the chairs 83/79/83).
- **A has its own material set:** streaky chimney wall, procedural plank floor, boucle sofa, crackle bark, smooth leather.

So A to C is roughly "same light, better materials", and C to B is roughly "same materials, different light and colour". That is relevant to the owner's question (see the end).

## Problems all three share

These hold every version below about 6.5, whatever the materials:

1. **The windows are not the light source.** In `wall.jpg` the clerestory panes are milky grey panels (mean 177/172/169 in all three), about as bright as the ceiling next to them and darker than it in B (192/169/142). In a sunny room the glass should be the brightest thing in frame by a wide margin. The pane edges also show jagged white stair-step aliasing along the top of each pane.
2. **The fire light leaks through the hearth.** `fire.jpg`, lower centre: an orange pool lights the floor right at the foot of the hearth's front face, where the hearth itself should throw that floor into shadow. Most visible in B because its oak is glossier.
3. **Things do not sit on the floor.** `chairs.jpg`: the four chair legs have no contact shadow at all, and the plinths only a faint one. The planter in `olive.jpg` and the frame above the fire in `wall.jpg` barely cast anything either. The frame reads as a sticker.
4. **The black vase is a hole.** `sofa.jpg` and `floor.jpg`, centre: mean 6/5/11, no reflection of the windows or the fire, no glaze highlight. A glazed vase in this room would carry two or three bright reflections.
5. **Props read as game assets.** Logs (`fire.jpg`, left of the firebox) are identical cylinders in a perfect pyramid with outlined end caps. The coffee table (`floor.jpg`, centre) has a low-resolution travertine top with repeating pill-shaped pits and vertical streaks down the sides where the texture is stretched. The sofa cushions are uniform rounded blocks with no creases or sag. The olive's branches meet the trunk with visible stepped collars (`olive.jpg`, centre, about 45% down).
6. **Dust motes read as dead pixels in a still.** White specks across the lower left floor of `11-living.jpg` in all three. They may work in motion. In a still they look like noise.
7. **The rug is close to black** in shadow (`floor.jpg`, left of the table, about 35 to 46 on the red channel), so it only shows its weave where the sun hits it.

What all three get right: the sun patch through the window crossing the table, the rug and the left sofa seat in `floor.jpg` has a crisp, believable shape and reveals the jute weave nicely. The fire glow on the chimney breast and across the hearth top (`fire.jpg`) falls off convincingly. The diagonal sun streak on the right-hand wall corner in `wall.jpg` is a good, photographic detail.

## A: 4 overall

Materials 4. Light 4.5. Premium 4.

**Strongest**
1. `sofa.jpg`, right half: the only version where the sofa reads as boucle. Real relief on backs and seats, plus stitched piping along the seat cushion edges. The scale is too coarse (more pebbled stucco than wool) but the intent reads.
2. `11-living.jpg`, lower left floor: individual boards have their own tone, so at walk distance it still reads as a plank floor (C loses this).
3. `chairs.jpg`, near chair: smooth cognac leather with a visible stitch line along the seat front reads as a sewn, aniline-type leather at a distance.

**Weakest**
1. `fire.jpg`, whole chimney breast and hearth: horizontal smeared streaks in blue-mauve. It reads as stained plywood or streaky paint, not board-marked concrete. The hearth top edge has a dotted "zipper" highlight.
2. `fire.jpg` and `chairs.jpg`, floor: every plank seam is a dotted, aliased line, and the grain is a stretched streak with cartoon concentric rings. Under the chairs it is a flat blue-grey. Logs in `fire.jpg` are glossy black tubes with flat brown end discs and black outlines. They look like toys.
3. `olive.jpg`, centre and top: the bark is a cell pattern with dark outlines (crocodile skin, not olive). The leaves are tiny, grey and sparse with sparkling aliased highlights in the top of the canopy, so the tree reads as dead or dried.

Light note: same lilac cast as C (ceiling and plaster blue-shifted), which fights the warm sun and fire.

**Single change that would most improve A:** replace the chimney wall, floor, bark and log materials. Those four surfaces are most of what makes A look like a game. With identical light, C (which has better versions of exactly these) scores a full point higher.

## B: 6 overall

Materials 6. Light 5.5. Premium 5.5.

**Strongest**
1. `chairs.jpg`, lower half: the oak floor is the best surface in the whole set. Real cathedral figure, plank-to-plank variation and a soft oiled sheen. The walnut chair plinths have believable grain too.
2. `fire.jpg`, chimney breast and hearth: board-marked concrete with board seams, tie holes, chips and colour variation. The fire light rakes across it convincingly to the upper right of the firebox.
3. `11-living.jpg` and `olive.jpg`: one coherent warm light. The ceiling picks up a warm bounce that is plausible off an oak floor in sun. The olive canopy is full, with backlit translucent leaves at the top of `olive.jpg`.

**Weakest**
1. `wall.jpg`, top half: the ceiling is a flat, saturated peach-tan with no plaster texture, and the clerestory glass is darker than the ceiling it should be lighting. Together with the beige sofa and tan floor, the whole frame drifts towards a sepia monotone with no clean white anywhere.
2. `fire.jpg`, lower centre: the strongest version of the hearth light leak (bright orange pool at the hearth's foot). In `chairs.jpg` the chair legs float with no contact shadow.
3. `chairs.jpg`, both chairs: the pebble grain is too large and too even for a lounge chair, and the oxblood colour carries almost no sheen on the rolled seat front or arm tops, so it reads as dark textured vinyl from a distance. In `olive.jpg` the leaves are a saturated yellow-green (bay or willow, not olive) and the black planter has pale flecks that read as scuffed plastic.

**Single change that would most improve B:** make the clerestory glass the brightest thing in the room (a real sky behind it, HDR bright with a little bloom) and let that drive a more neutral bounce on the ceiling. Right now the room is lit by nothing you can see, which is a big part of the "baked" feeling.

## C: 5 overall

Materials 5.5. Light 4.5. Premium 5.

**Strongest**
1. `chairs.jpg`, near chair: the best leather of the three. Cognac with a pebble grain and the most specular variation along the seat roll and arm tops.
2. `fire.jpg`: the same board-marked concrete as B, here closer to raw concrete grey, with the warm fire glow standing out against it.
3. `olive.jpg`, canopy: darker, more muted green than B, closer to an olive, still with the fibrous bark. The plaster behind it has a fine speckle that reads as a real trowelled finish.

**Weakest**
1. `11-living.jpg`, lower left, and `chairs.jpg`, floor: the oak is graded to a grey-violet (83/79/83). Up close it is a plausible fumed oak, but at walk distance the plank seams vanish and it reads like grey carpet or screed.
2. `11-living.jpg` and `wall.jpg`: lilac ceiling and plaster plus a blue-purple concrete (64/64/73). Against the warm sun streaks and the fire this reads as dusk or a wrong white balance, not a sunny afternoon.
3. `sofa.jpg`, right half: the upholstery is a plain fine weave, so the boucle is gone and the long sofa reads as one flat beige block. The shared problems (hearth leak, floating chair legs, dull windows, black-hole vase, toy logs) all apply here too.

**Single change that would most improve C:** fix the white balance. Neutral warm-white ceiling and plaster, true grey concrete, and a floor that stays recognisably oak at distance. The materials are already B's, so this alone should bring it close to B.

---

## On the owner's question: will better materials alone fix it?

From these pictures, partly. A to C is close to a materials-only change (the light is measurably the same), and it is worth about one point: from obvious CG to a decent real-time render. B over C is a light and colour change on the same materials, worth about another point.

What still separates B from an 8 is mostly not materials: windows that are not light sources, missing contact shadows, the fire light passing through the hearth, a vase with no reflections, and blocky prop geometry (logs, sofa cushions, branch collars). Better textures will not fix those. The next point has to come from light and grounding, then from a few hero props with real geometry.
