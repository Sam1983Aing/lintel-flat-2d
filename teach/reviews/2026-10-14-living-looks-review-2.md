# Living room looks, blind review 2

14 October 2026. Outside reviewer, judged from the pictures only (folders X and Y, seven views each, 3456 x 1984). I didn't open any other project files.

| Criterion | X | Y |
|---|---|---|
| Materials | 4 | 6 |
| Light | 5 | 5.5 |
| Premium feel | 4.5 | 6 |
| **Overall** | **4.5** | **6** |

Pick: **Y**.

Anchors used: 3 obvious CG, 5 decent real-time render, 7 convincing at a glance with flaws on inspection, 8 reads as a photographed room in most places, 10 indistinguishable from a photograph.

## What the two have in common

The light rig looks identical. Sun comes through the clerestory and lands as a soft-edged patch on the left sofa and the rug, the fire throws a warm pool on the hearth and the floor, and the same dust motes float in both. Average brightness per view is within about 2 points out of 255 between X and Y on every view I measured. So the half point Y gets on light comes from how its surfaces respond to the same lamps (concrete relief catching the firelight, floor sheen picking up the glow under the hearth). It doesn't come from better lighting.

Shared problems, which hold both of them back the same amount:

- **Shadow side goes black.** The rug outside the sun patch measures roughly 14 to 32 out of 255 and shows no weave at all (floor.jpg, right of the coffee table and lower right). The slots under the sofa plinths are pure black too. A real room this sunny would bounce warm light off the oak into those areas.
- **Ceiling is a flat beige plane.** No gradient from the windows, no fittings, no shadow line at the clerestory (wall.jpg, top half).
- **Clerestory glass is opaque white** with no sky or exterior behind it, and there's a jagged bright line along the top edge of the panes (wall.jpg, top right, zoomed).
- **The fire is a bright sprite.** There's no glass reflection on the firebox front and the flame is the same height all along (fire.jpg).
- **Geometry is blocky.** Sofa and chair silhouettes have faceted corner chamfers (chairs.jpg, top corners of the right chair back). The books and candle are plain boxes and a cylinder with no bevels (chairs.jpg side table, floor.jpg by the vase). The coffee table sides are a vertically stretched texture (floor.jpg, front face of the table).
- **The planter** is the same black tub with flecks in both, and it's fine.

## X

### Scores
Materials 4, light 5, premium 4.5, overall 4.5.

### Three strongest
1. **Floor plank layout** (11-living.jpg lower left, chairs.jpg middle). The boards are staggered properly at the butt joints and each one has its own tone, which reads as laid flooring.
2. **Sofa reads as boucle** (floor.jpg, sunlit seat on the left). The nubby surface catches the sun patch, and the seat cushions have a welt seam with stitching (sofa.jpg, lower right cushions).
3. **Sun patch and firelight** (floor.jpg left sofa and rug, fire.jpg floor in front of the hearth). Soft penumbra and a warm pool. Same as Y, but it's the best thing in the frame.

### Three weakest
1. **The chimney breast concrete** (wall.jpg left, fire.jpg whole wall, 11-living.jpg centre). It reads as grey marbled veneer or brushed paint with pale wavy smears. There's no formwork and no pitting. This is the focal wall of the room, so it costs the most.
2. **Olive bark** (olive.jpg trunk and branches). The bark is a cell pattern of polygons outlined in dark lines, so the trunk reads as crocodile skin or dried mud.
3. **Logs and leather chairs read as plastic.** The logs are glossy black tubes with a toon-style outline and a flat end cap (fire.jpg left of the firebox). The chairs are smooth satin rust with faint vertical streaks and no grain or seams (chairs.jpg right chair), and the chair base is plain brown plastic.

Also seen: a dotted bright seam along the hearth's front edge (fire.jpg, top arris of the hearth, right half). Floor joints render as saw-tooth dotted lines in places (fire.jpg bottom right, chairs.jpg step edge on the left). There are faint looping line marks in the plaster behind the olive that look like a doodle overlay (olive.jpg, wall right of the trunk). The olive canopy sparkles with aliased specular glints (olive.jpg top, 11-living.jpg tree).

## Y

### Scores
Materials 6, light 5.5, premium 6, overall 6.

### Three strongest
1. **Board-formed concrete** (fire.jpg, wall.jpg, 11-living.jpg centre). Horizontal formwork lines, tie holes, pitting, lighter and darker pours, a hairline crack on the wall left of the firebox. The firelight grazes it and shows the relief. This one change does most of the work in the walk view.
2. **Oak floor figure** (fire.jpg lower right, chairs.jpg foreground). Cathedral grain, knots and flake that read as oiled oak. The sheen carries the warm glow from under the hearth across the boards, which is the most photographic moment in either set.
3. **Leather and linen** (chairs.jpg, sofa.jpg). The chairs have a pebbled grain that reads as leather at a glance. The mustard throw and the bolster have a visible weave, so they read as linen.

### Three weakest
1. **Floor has lost its planks** (chairs.jpg middle, 11-living.jpg lower left). The joints are nearly invisible and the boards don't vary in tone, so it reads as one wide photograph laid flat. At walk distance there are white streak flecks that look like noise more than wear.
2. **Sofa crease artefact and identity** (sofa.jpg middle right, along the bottom of each back cushion). There's a pale sliver with a stair-stepped edge where the back cushion meets the seat. The cushions have lost their welts. And the fabric reads as fine linen or tweed, not boucle.
3. **Olive is still not an olive** (olive.jpg). The bark is reddish and fissured like pine. Real olive is grey and twisted, with smoother patches. The leaves are a saturated bright green rather than olive's silver-grey. There are stepped collars at every branch join, and you can see them in X too.

Also seen: the leather grain is over-scaled and the same everywhere, more bison than a fine aniline. It also smears on the front faces of the arms (chairs.jpg right chair, arm ends). The chair base is rough-sawn rustic timber, which reads as wood but fights the clean form. The log end grain uses the same radial check mark on every log and all logs are identical cylinders (fire.jpg). Under firelight the hearth top reads close to timber rather than concrete (fire.jpg, hearth top right of the firebox).

## Which I'd show a client

I'd show Y, because the two surfaces a client looks at first, the fireplace wall and the floor, read as real materials in Y and as CG in X.

## Single change that would most improve each

- **X:** replace the chimney breast and hearth with a proper board-formed concrete set (colour, normal and roughness from a real scan). It's the focal wall and the main thing that makes X read as a game level.
- **Y:** put real bounce light into the shadow side. Lift the rug, the slots under the sofas and the sofa fronts from black to a warm deep brown, and give the ceiling a gradient from the sunlit floor. Every material in Y is now ahead of the light, and the crushed shadows are what still say real-time. Restoring plank joints on the floor comes a close second.
