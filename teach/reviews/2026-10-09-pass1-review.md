# Lintel · Casa Serra. Outside review of pass 1

Date: 9 October 2026
Reviewer: outside creative director (did not see the builder's own notes)
Material: the BEFORE stills, the AFTER stills of pass 1, three of the client's own screenshots (sam-08, sam-09, sam-10) and the flicker numbers supplied with the brief. All stills are in `teach/shots/`.

How I scored. 8 means a paying client who asked for premium would take that part with no notes. 5 is average. I judged against good real-time web work, not against an offline render. Motion cannot be seen in a still, so for flicker I used the supplied numbers and looked in the stills for the things that usually cause it.

Short version: every one of the seven items is clearly better than before. None of them is at 8 yet.

---

## 1. Plan lines and the opening

**After: 7 / 10. Before: 5 / 10.**

The hero plan is much cleaner. In `before/00-hero` the west walls were thin and broken and the outer wall changed weight from one stretch to the next. In `after/00-hero` solid walls are one heavy dark stroke and glazing is one thin line, so the drawing finally has a rule. In `after/03-lens2` the cut tops of the walls are a flat dark fill where before they were half outline and half smeared hatching. The flicker numbers agree at the start of the scroll (2.65 down to 0.51 at 36 vh, 2.18 down to 0.65 at 70 vh). The middle of the opening is a different story: 2.37 at 120 vh and 3.02 at 170 vh is only about a third better than before, and that is exactly the stretch the client called "blinking".

Fixes, most valuable first:

1. Calm the middle of the opening (120 to 170 vh). The stills show the likely cause. In `03-lens2` the hairlines on the garage slab and on the entry path below the door break into rows of dots, and in `04-lens3` the roof edges and the glass hatching pile up into a dense comb along the horizon. Give thin lines a minimum width on screen (about 1.25 px) and fade the paving grid and the hatch by distance and by viewing angle so nothing drops below a pixel.
2. Labels are hidden under furniture on the very first frame. PRIVATE GARDEN has lost its first two letters and its area figure under the new L sofa and a faint dashed circle. It was fully readable in `before/00-hero`, so this one is worse than before. The bath's "48 m²" now sits under the vanity symbol. PATIO is still cut in half by the bench. The kitchen and entrance area figures are clipped by the table and a wall. Move each label to clear floor or knock out the linework behind it.
3. In `01-scroll1` the room counter fades in on top of the plan's "8.00" and "3.00" figures at bottom right, and the small logo lands on top of the "LINTEL · CASA SERRA, SINTRA · 2026" line at top left. Shift one of each pair or delay it.
4. The garage reads lighter than the rest of the house in `03-lens2`. Its walls have no dark cut top and their edges are dotted. Cap them like the other walls or draw the open side as a deliberate dashed line.

## 2. The scan follows the camera (the patio after the dining room)

**After: 7.5 / 10. Before: 3 / 10.**

This is the best answer of the round. In `before/13-slide` and in the client's sam-08 the whole patio was simply there when the camera turned. Now `13-turn-to-patio` shows paper and ink through the glass while the kitchen behind is finished, `14-slide` catches the orange front crossing the paving toward the pool, and at the stop in `15-patio` the gallery wing across the court is still unbuilt. The idea "it scans as we walk" now reads in three stills in a row. What keeps it under 8 is the order things turn real and a couple of raw edges inside the unscanned part.

Fixes, most valuable first:

1. Scan strictly by distance from the camera. In `14-slide` the lawn, the stone wall, the cypresses and the sky are fully real while the loungers and the umbrella standing in front of them are still paper. The olive's crown is finished while its own planter ring is mid-scan. Far things finished before near things breaks the logic the client asked for.
2. Unscanned trees are flat white cut-outs with ragged edges: the small tree against the sky at centre right and the white tuft inside the olive in `14-slide`. They look like holes in the picture. Give them an ink outline like everything else, or hide them until the front reaches them.
3. In `15-patio` the gallery wing is blank white panels inside bronze frames that are already real. It reads as boarded up. Put ink linework on those panels (the frames on the wall, the benches) and let the bronze mullions arrive with the room, not ahead of it.
4. Plan figures hang in the finished scene: "24." is cut off by the dark wall and "18.00" sits over the pool in `14-slide` and `15-patio`. Fade each figure the moment its area turns real.
5. The orange grid on the paving in `14-slide` is dense and very saturated. Thin it or shorten its life so it reads as a passing front and not as a neon floor.

## 3. The gallery

**After: 6.5 / 10. Before: 4 / 10.**

Half of the complaint is fixed. In `before/17-gallery` and sam-09 every artwork down the corridor was visible on arrival. In `after/18-gallery` only the first few metres are real and the rest of the corridor is ink with the scan grid on ceiling and floor, and `19-galleryMid` shows the front further down. The other half is not fixed. The "rough edges on the top right" are still in the picture: in `19-galleryMid` the shadow where the ceiling meets the head of the glazing is the same stair-stepped ragged band as in `before/18-galleryMid`.

Fixes, most valuable first:

1. Remove the stepped shadow band on the ceiling along the glazing head (top right of `19-galleryMid`). It is a shadow map that is too coarse for a long thin room. Fit the shadow area tightly to the corridor, raise its resolution and add a little bias, or stop the ceiling from receiving that shadow and paint a soft gradient there instead. The same ragged edge is on the kitchen ceiling beam in `12-dining` and `13-turn-to-patio`, so one fix should cure both.
2. The front of the frame is too soft in `18-gallery`. The first artwork, the bronze spheres and the potted plant are all blurred and only a thin slice in the distance is sharp. A gallery where the art is out of focus feels wrong. Pull focus nearer or halve the blur at this stop.
3. Orange scan bars lie across things that are already real and close to the camera: a band around the bronze spheres and bars across the two framed pieces on the left in `18-gallery`. Keep the orange on the advancing front only.
4. "24.00" and "3.60" float in the middle of the corridor. Keep them on the paper part and drop them once the scan has passed.
5. The room title sits on top of the blurred artwork with its rule lines crossing the frame. Give it a calm patch of wall.

## 4. The master bedroom

**After: 6.5 / 10. Before: 4 / 10.**

A big step. `before/21-bedroom` was a mattress on a plinth with two pillow blocks, globe pendants and wall-hung nightstands, seen from far away. `after/22-bedroom` moves in close and adds a full-width leather headboard with a lit shelf, styled objects, table lamps on nightstands that now stand on the floor, an upholstered bench, a curtain and low sun across the rug. The furniture is grounded now. It is still not a high-end bedroom, because the things that sell a bedroom are the soft ones and those are still stiff.

Fixes, most valuable first:

1. The bedding is rigid. The folded top sheet is a hard white board and its right corner sticks out past the mattress in mid-air (the one thing still "floating"). The mustard throw hangs like a folded card. The two back pillows stand up as stiff slabs. Give the soft goods thickness, sag and a few creases, tuck the sheet in and let the throw drape.
2. The lamp shades are blown out. Both shades are pure white cut-outs and the ceramic bases are nearly as flat. Lower the glow until the shade shows a warm gradient, and let the light pool on the headboard and on the nightstand top.
3. The room title cannot be read. Dark stencil letters sit on the black timber wall and the subline has vanished (top right of `22-bedroom`). Switch the title block to paper white whenever it sits over a dark area. The same fault is at the olive patio.
4. The black wall is a flat void with white scratch marks. The right third of the frame is dead. Graze it softly from the linear light so the boards show relief, and cut the white speckle by half.
5. A plan figure, "3.60", hangs in front of the window view. Remove it once the room is real.
6. The timber floor reads pink to mauve. Bring it back to a neutral warm oak.
7. The rug is a flat cream sheet with no edge and no pile, and the bed base nearly disappears into it. Give it a visible edge, a weave and a slightly deeper tone.
8. The grain on the nightstand is oversized, like a wood sticker. Scale it down to about a third.
9. The white specks in front of the window read as snow. Thin them out near glass.

## 5. The bath

**After: 7 / 10. Before: 4 / 10.**

The camera complaint is answered. `before/23-bath` showed a window, a wall pier in the middle of the frame and a tub cut off at the right edge. `after/26-bath-end` shows the tub, a long double vanity with mirror and sconces, the stone floor and the garden through the glass, all in one frame, and `25-bath-mid` catches the garden being scanned through the window during the stop, which is a nice touch. The room now reads as a room. One object spoils it.

Fixes, most valuable first:

1. The mirror shows a flat blue sky and nothing of the room. It reads as a blue screen on the wall and it is the first thing the eye lands on. Make it reflect the bathroom (the window, the tub, the back wall), even a simplified reflection would do.
2. Tidy the right edge of the frame. The plant is cut in half and collides with the progress ruler, an orange pot is clipped in the corner, and the wide lens tilted down makes the right side of the mirror and the right sconce lean. Level the camera or narrow the lens a little, then bring the plant fully in or take it out.
3. The three sconces are blown to pure white, and the warm glow on the wall above the mirror has no visible source. Dim the sconces to a warm gradient and tie the wall glow to a real fitting or remove it.
4. The walnut on the vanity has huge swirling grain and the stool looks like stacked plywood rings. Scale the textures down to about a third and add shadow gaps and pulls to the drawers.
5. A thin red dotted line runs down the edge of the white pier left of the glass in `26-bath-end`. It looks like a scan line that never cleared.
6. The bath mat is a flat card. Give it thickness and pile.
7. The plant's leaves are faceted blocks. Either soften them or keep that plant away from the camera.
8. The tub is a little flat. Add a contact shadow and a soft reflection of the window, and thicken the brass filler, which is a stick right now.

## 6. The private garden

**After: 6.5 / 10. Before: 2 / 10.**

I agree with the client's 2 for `before/24-garden`: blurred pool edge, bare dunes, nothing to look at. `after/27-garden` is a furnished terrace with a fire bowl, lounge chairs, sunbeds, a log stack, an olive in a planted bed, clipped balls, a pergola with a sofa, cypresses, and hills that now carry scrub and a white village above the water. It is no longer empty. It has swung the other way: many objects at equal weight, several of them toy-like, and the title block is a mess.

Fixes, most valuable first:

1. The foreground is the weakest part and it is the biggest. The fire bowl is out of focus and its embers are flat orange discs with no flame and no light on the chairs around it. A blurred chair back fills the bottom left corner. Either make the fire the hero (sharp, glowing, warm light spilling on the chairs) or push it to the middle distance and clear the corner.
2. Clean the title block in `27-garden`. The crosshair marker sits on the title between THE and PRIVATE GARDEN, a stray "21.00" runs through the subline, and the whole block is parked on top of the village. The same crosshair collision was in the BEFORE.
3. Two glitches: a dashed grey line hangs in the sky at top right, and the leaf mass on the pergola roof is pin sharp with pink fringe pixels while the olive beside it is soft.
4. The planting looks like toys. The clipped balls are perfect dark spheres with no leaf texture, the lavender is spiky purple stars, the olive trunk is pale and smooth like plaster. Texture the balls, make the lavender soft grey-violet mounds and darken and roughen the trunk.
5. The plunge pool reads as a grey concrete channel. Give it reflective water with the sky in it and a darker waterline. The title promises "the valley, then the sea", so the water should be the calm part of the frame, and right now the valley is squeezed into the left third behind the text.
6. The lawn is flat mustard felt. Make it greener with some mowing variation.
7. The hill on the right is faceted flat polygons while the hill on the left is smooth, and the scrub is evenly spaced dots. Smooth the right hill and cluster the scrub in uneven groups.
8. The sky is a flat grey-beige with the sun in frame. Give it a dusk gradient.
9. Choose one hero for the frame (fire with the valley beyond, or the olive with the pergola) and thin out the rest.

## 7. The ending

**After: 6.5 / 10. Before: 4 / 10.**

The idea the client asked for is on screen. `before/26-back2` pulled back to a tilted ink plan with stray orange boxes and then faded to the closing text. Now the camera lifts over the finished house (`28-lift`, `29-top-built`), a paper roll travels across it (`30-roll-1`, `31-roll-2`) and leaves the drawing behind in exact register with the building, and `32-sheet` is the hero plan again. The loop closing to within 0.1 grey levels of the first frame is real craft. The roll itself is not there yet, and the frame it rolls over is the muddiest of the whole walk.

Fixes, most valuable first:

1. Make the roll a real object. In `30-roll-1` and `31-roll-2` it is a plain cream cylinder with a soft gradient. It casts no shadow on the ground it is covering, its ends show no spiral of paper, the sheet has no thickness and no lifted edge, and nothing of the drawing shows on the tube. Add a contact shadow ahead of the roll, a visible paper edge, a faint reversed print of the plan on the outside of the tube and a small overshoot and settle when it stops.
2. The left 40 percent of the sheet is blank, so the first half of the unroll (`30-roll-1`) reveals empty paper. Unroll from the plan side, or top to bottom, or start with the tube already at the plan's left edge, so ink appears at once.
3. The client said "a plan rolled into a tube". We never see the tube as an object before it starts wiping. Give it one beat: the tube lying across the site at a slight angle, a pause, then it opens.
4. `29-top-built` is the last look at the finished house and it is dark and muddy: mustard-brown ground with a blotchy repeating pattern, heavy black shadows, the house small in the frame, trees as tiny dots, thin violet lines along the wall tops. Brighten it, turn the ground greener, soften the shadows and frame tighter on the house.
5. The nav links and the room counter are dark text on dark ground in `28-lift`, `29-top-built` and `30-roll-1` and cannot be read.
6. Check the closing screen. `before/27-end` had a START A PROJECT button, a SEE ALL WORK link, the outline LINTEL wordmark and a footer line. I cannot see them in `33-finale`. If the still was simply taken earlier in the scroll, fine. If they were dropped, put them back. In the same still the headline runs across the faded garage and pool linework, so fade the plan a touch more behind the text.

---

## Overall

Pass 1 answered all seven notes in substance, and that is worth saying plainly: the plan has a line logic, the scan really does travel with the camera at the patio and down the gallery, the bath camera shows the room, the bedroom and the garden are furnished, and the ending is the paper roll he described. What separates this from an 8 is finish, and the same few faults repeat from room to room. Soft things are stiff (bedding, throws, mats). Bright things are blown out (lamp shades, sconces, the uplit olive trunk). Plan figures and crosshair markers drift through finished rooms and collide with the titles, and the titles themselves cannot be read on dark backgrounds. A coarse shadow leaves a ragged edge on two ceilings. Wood grain is scaled too large. Exterior ground is mustard where it should be green. Fix those as systems and four or five of the seven items move up together. The single item furthest from its promise is the gallery, because the rough edge the client pointed at is still in the still. The best item is the patio scan.
