## The same in all three

**The sheet.** One camera for the whole page. At the top it hangs more than a kilometre straight above the house with a lens of about 2 degrees, so nothing has perspective and it reads as a printed plan. Scrolling brings that same camera down while the lens opens to about 45 degrees. No cut, no swap from a picture to a model.

**The cut.** A clipping plane at 1.2 m. Cut walls are filled solid black, the way an architect inks them. Everything below the cut is outline only. Room names, dimension strings and door swings are drawn on the floor of the model, so they are still under your feet when you walk in.

**The rise.** "Walls rising" is that cut plane lifting from 1.2 m to above the roof. One thing to know: from straight above, a rising wall looks exactly like a cut wall. That is what makes the sheet honest, and it also means the rise only shows once the camera tips. So the camera starts tipping at the moment the cut starts lifting, and the roof closes overhead just as you reach the door.

**House and light.** Single storey. Sun low in the west, long warm bars across the floors. Palette unless you say otherwise: pale limestone floors, oak, lime plaster, a travertine island, linen and leather, a little bronze. Each plan also gets a bedroom wing so the sheet reads as a whole house. Its walls rise with the rest, but the camera never goes in and it stays a line drawing.

**The first four stops are identical in every direction**

1. The sheet. Camera still, straight above. Paper, ink, room names, dimensions, north arrow, scale bar, title block.
2. The lift. The cut plane rises, the camera tips back and falls toward the front of the house. Walls come up out of their own ink.
3. The forecourt. Eye height (1.6 m), 8 m from the front door. Still paper and black line, now in perspective.
4. The door and the hall. The door opens, the camera crosses the threshold, materials sweep across the hall away from you.

After that come the living room, the kitchen and the courtyard. That is where the three differ. The sketches are schematic, the sizes in the text are the real ones.
